Background and course rationale: The study of Ukrainian history in schools tends to focus more on answering the questions 'Who?', 'Where?', and 'When?' rather than 'Why?'. For instance, anyone who has studied it can recall who attacked the Ukrainians, when it happened, and how many times throughout history. However, they often can't explain why the Ukrainians survived these challenges. As a result, the way Ukrainian history is taught in secondary schools isn't sufficient to fully understand the key historical processes.
Course aims to supplement the general list of historical names and events with an exploration of their causes and consequences, which will lead to a better understanding of both the historical processes in modern Ukraine and its current situation.
Prerequisites: basic knowledge of Ukrainian history and the sequence of main events can save you some time in this course, but they are not crucial prerequisites. You will still be able to begin and participate in the course without this background.
Learning outcomes:
* Students should rekindle/develop their interest in Ukrainian history.
* Students will be able to identify the reasons and consequences of key events and processes in modern Ukrainian history.
* Students will be able to analyze historical documents and data.
* Students will be able to discuss and explain modern Ukrainian history.
* Students will be able to reflect on the content of historical books.
How we will work at this course (what kind of tasks and activities students are expected do): 
During this course, students will attend classes, read assigned literature or watch assigned videos, participate in seminar discussions, take quizzes, write reflection papers, and pass a final exam.
Lectures and seminars
This course consists of 14 lectures and 10 seminars. 
Lectures will be held on campus with recordings. The video of each lecture will be uploaded to the Moodle course page within 2-3 working days after the lecture.
Seminars will take place on campus.
Attendance: Students are strongly encouraged to attend all lectures and seminars.
Lecture Format: Lectures will include occasional short discussions initiated by the lecturer to maintain an interactive environment. Each lecture will be supplemented with additional readings and videos. These materials are not just a recap of the lecture content but are designed to address topics that may not have been covered in the lecture. It is crucial to read the assigned literature and watch the videos no later than 1-2 days after each lecture to avoid being overwhelmed with material before each seminar.
Seminars will begin with a 15 minute quiz to assess your understanding of the assigned reading material and the corresponding lectures. The quiz will include four open-ended questions, and students must choose only one to answer. The use of sources (Google, Wikipedia, AI-tools, literature, personal notes etc.) or external help is not allowed during the quiz. The quiz can only be taken in the classroom. Students who take the quiz outside of the classroom will receive zero points. Each quiz will be followed by a brief feedback session on the correct answers.

Quizzes assess how well students have processed both the mandatory reading materials and the lectures themselves. Each quiz is worth a maximum of 4 points: 2 points for the completeness of the answer and 2 points for correctness. A complete answer is one that fully covers the question and all its components. Correctness is evaluated using a penalty system, with 0.5 points deducted from the maximum 2 points for each instance of incorrect judgments or factual inaccuracies. The final quiz score is rounded to the nearest whole number: 0.5 rounds up to 1, 1.5 to 2, 2.5 to 3, and 3.5 to 4.
Preparation and Participation: Please ensure that you have read the assigned literature before each seminar and are prepared to discuss it. Seminars are student-driven discussions, guided by the lecturer or TAs. During the seminar, the lecturer/TAs will assess each student’s level of participation in meaningful discussion and the accuracy of their answers.
Reflection paper 
1. The list of books (chapters) and films for the reflection papers is provided below.
2. Selection Process: By no later than Friday of the third week, students should select a book or film for their reflection paper and inform the lecturer or TA of their choice.
3. Reading Requirement: Students must read the chosen book before writing their reflection paper.
4. Reflection Paper Guidelines: A reflection paper should address the following questions:
* What historical event or period does the book cover?
* What is the main idea or ideas presented by the author? What are the key arguments?
* Why was this text written? What circumstances motivated the author?
* How persuasive is the author in conveying their opinion?
* Did the book change your previous perspective? If so, how?
* What parallels or similarities with our current time did you notice?
* How would you critique the author's opinion? What do you disagree with, and why?
5. Film Reflection Paper (Optional): If you choose to write an additional reflection paper on a film, please answer the following questions:
* What historical event or period does the film cover?
* What is the director’s main idea?
* What impact did the film have on its audience? How significant was it? What were the critical reviews?
* How does the film relate to the ongoing transformations in Ukrainian collective memory?
6. Important Note: An additional reflection paper on a film can only be written after submitting a reflection paper on a book. Students cannot substitute a book reflection paper with a film reflection paper.
7. Evidence and Citations: Students must provide evidence to support their arguments. Whenever literature or another source is used, it must be accompanied by an in-text citation (including the author and page number). It is in the students’ best interest to clearly distinguish their own thoughts from those of other authors, using MLA citation style.
8. Individual Work: The reflection paper must be the student's own individual work. It is not allowed to use assistance from others or any AI tools in the creation of your reflection paper.
9. Assessment Criteria: When assessing a reflection paper, the lecturer or TA will consider how thoroughly students address the questions mentioned above, how well they argue their points, their familiarity with the topic, and the depth of their analysis.
10. Length and Submission: The reflection paper should be between 6000-9000 characters (2000-3000 characters for an additional reflection paper), excluding spaces. The majority of the reflection paper should consist of the student’s own thoughts, conclusions, and arguments.
11. Deadlines:
* The deadline for reflection papers is October 22nd (23:59 Kyiv time). Reflection papers should be uploaded here. Please title the file as "Reflection Paper [Your Surname]."
* The deadline for additional reflection papers is October 30th (23:59 Kyiv time). Additional reflection papers should be uploaded here. Please title the file as "Additional Reflection Paper [Your Surname]."
12. Feedback: Students will receive oral feedback from their TAs or lecturer on their reflection papers during office hours or exam sessions.
Final exam 
The final exam will be conducted orally on campus. Each student will receive 5 broad questions and must thoroughly answer at least 2 of them to demonstrate a deep understanding of the material for maximum points. Students can choose which 2 questions to answer. After receiving the questions, students will have 15 minutes to organize their thoughts before presenting their answers. The use of sources (Google, Wikipedia, AI-tools, literature, personal notes etc.) or external help is not allowed during the exam. 
If academic integrity violations persist during the course, the final exam format will be changed from oral to written. Students will have one hour to handwrite their answers on paper or type them in a safe browser mode.
Grading Policy:
* Quizzes: 40%
* Reflection Paper: 20%
* Participation in Discussions: 20%
* Final Exam: 20%
* Extra Credit: 10% for an additional reflection paper on a film.
Points Breakdown:
* 10 quizzes, maximum 4 points each.
* 10 seminar discussions, maximum 2 points each.
* 1 reflection paper on a book/chapter, maximum 20 points.
* 1 additional reflection paper on a film, maximum 10 points.
* 1 final exam, maximum 20 points.
Additional Reflection Paper: Students have the option to write one additional reflection paper if they are less comfortable participating in seminar discussions or wish to improve their grade. For instance, if a student doesn’t wish to or cannot participate in 3 seminar discussions, they can write one additional reflection paper to substitute their participation grade. However, quizzes cannot be substituted by reflection papers.
Late Submissions: Reflection papers submitted after the deadline (even 1 minute later) will receive at most 50% of the full grade. Submissions more than 4 days past the deadline will receive a zero grade, though you may still submit the paper to receive feedback on your work.
Make-up Exams: There will be no make-up exams.
AI and Assistance Policy: It is not permitted to use assistance from others or any AI tools in the creation of your reflection paper.

Seminar 8. The Sixtiers’ riot against the empire? Negotiating the change in the 1960s
In Soviet Ukraine, the 1960s are firmly associated with the Sixtiers, a cohort of creative and humanitarian intelligentsia who challenged the limits of permissible national expression in art as well as in public life. Students will participate in a moderated discussion to explore the phenomenon of Khrushchev’s Thaw and its peculiarities as compared to Stalinism in terms of social and cultural politics. Questions to discuss:
   * How would you define the limits of permissible action in the cultural sphere in Ukrainian SSR of the 1960s? 
   * What agenda did the Sixtiers have? 
   * How did they try to negotiate the change and how effective they were in their aspirations?
Mandatory readings:
   * Радомир Мокрик. Малесенька щопта проти імперії зла
   * Іван Дзюба. Пояснювальна записка про вечір пам’яті Лесі Українки 31 липня 1963 року. Сучасність. 1968. № 8 (92). С. 87-94.
